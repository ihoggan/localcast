# Part P in plain words

[The Maker's own notes: common domestic jobs in everyday words, each pointing to the section of Approved Document P (2013 edition) that covers it. A summary, not the regulations. If this and AD P ever disagree, AD P wins.]

## Do I need to tell building control? The short answer

Only three kinds of job need notifying (AD P 2.5): a new circuit from the fuse box, changing the fuse box (consumer unit) itself, and adding to or altering circuits right next to a bath or shower. Everything else is not notifiable (AD P 2.7). Notifying means building control, or a registered competent person or third-party certifier who does it for you (AD P 3.1).

## Adding a socket, a light or a spur

Adding a socket, a spur, a light or a switch to a circuit that's already there is NOT notifiable, in any room except close to a bath or shower (AD P 2.7). It still has to be done properly to BS 7671 (AD P 1.6, 1.7, 3.13). If it needs its own new circuit back to the fuse box, it IS notifiable (AD P 2.5).

## Changing or replacing a fuse box (consumer unit)

Replacing a fuse box or consumer unit IS notifiable, even like for like (AD P 2.5). Don't be caught out by AD P 2.7: it says replacements are not notifiable, but that means replacing other things, such as a socket, a light fitting or a switch. The consumer unit is the exception, listed in AD P 2.5.

## Replacing, repairing or maintaining things

Swapping a socket, switch or light fitting for a new one, repairs and maintenance are NOT notifiable, anywhere in the house, even in a bathroom (AD P 2.7). The one exception is the fuse box (consumer unit), which is notifiable (AD P 2.5).

## New circuits: cookers, showers, outdoor supplies

Any job that needs a new circuit from the fuse box is notifiable (AD P 2.5): for example a new cooker circuit, a supply for an electric shower, or a new supply to a garage, shed or garden office. Fitting a built-in cooker on an existing circuit is not notifiable (AD P 2.8). Wiring an electric gate or garage door to an existing isolator is not notifiable, but a new circuit to the isolator is (AD P 2.8).

## Bathrooms and showers

Near a bath or shower is a "special location" (AD P 2.5, 2.6): from the edge of the bath or shower tray out to 0.6 m, and from the floor up to 2.25 m. With no tray, it's 1.2 m from the shower head. Adding to or altering a circuit inside that zone IS notifiable. Sockets should not be within 3 m of a bath or shower tray (AD P 2.6). Rooms with a swimming pool or sauna heater count too (AD P 2.5).

## Outside, gardens, sheds and garages

Part P covers work outside the house: outside lights, pond pumps, garden lighting, solar panels on the roof, and sheds, garages, greenhouses and conservatories that get their electricity from the house (AD P 2.2, 2.3). Whether it's notifiable follows the usual rule: a new circuit is, adding to an existing one isn't (AD P 2.5, 2.7).

## Flats, shops and businesses

Part P covers the shared corridors, stairs, laundries and gyms in blocks of flats, and a shop or pub under a flat if it shares the flat's meter (AD P 2.2). It does NOT cover business premises with their own meter, or the power supply to lifts in blocks of flats (AD P 2.3).

## Who signs it off, and the paperwork

Three ways to certify notifiable work (AD P 3.1): a registered competent person self-certifies (AD P 3.3); an unregistered installer uses a registered third-party certifier, arranged before work starts (AD P 3.5); or the installer tells building control before work starts (AD P 3.8). The customer gets a BS 7671 electrical installation certificate, and within 30 days the occupier gets a Building Regulations compliance certificate (AD P 3.3, 3.4, 3.7). Building control issues a completion certificate instead (AD P 3.12).

## Do I have to bring the old wiring up to standard?

No. When adding to or altering an installation, only the new work must meet current standards (AD P 1.6), unless the new work makes the old installation less safe, or the old installation is too poor for the new work to run safely. Check the existing earthing, bonding and the supply can take the extra load first (AD P 1.7). Old cable colours don't need replacing if the cable is adequate (AD P 1.5).

## Heights of sockets and switches in new houses

In new houses only: sockets and switches between 450 mm and 1200 mm from the floor, and consumer unit switches between 1350 mm and 1450 mm, out of reach of small children (AD P 1.4, from Approved Document M).

## Labels, certificates and handover information

The customer should be given what they need to use and maintain the installation safely: the electrical installation certificate, permanent labels (earth connections, bonds, the consumer unit, RCDs), operating instructions, and plans only for big or complex jobs (AD P 1.2).
