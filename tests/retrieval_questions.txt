## document-worded

Q: When did the 2013 edition of Approved Document P take effect for use in England?
ANSWER: 6 April 2013.
EXPECT: AD P Main changes in the 2013 edition

Q: What edition of BS 7671 does the 2013 approved document refer to for electrical installations?
ANSWER: BS 7671:2008 incorporating Amendment No 1:2011.
EXPECT: AD P Main changes in the 2013 edition
EXPECT: AD P 1.1 Design and installation: General

Q: What are the two primary categories of changes introduced in the 2013 edition of Approved Document P?
ANSWER: Changes in the legal requirements and changes in the technical guidance.
EXPECT: AD P Main changes in the 2013 edition

Q: What is the primary objective of Requirement P1 under Part P of the Building Regulations?
ANSWER: Reasonable provision in design and installation to protect people operating, maintaining or altering installations from fire or injury.
EXPECT: AD P Requirement P1: Design and installation

Q: What voltage ranges must electrical installations operate at for Part P requirements to apply?
ANSWER: Low voltage or extra-low voltage.
EXPECT: AD P Requirement P1: Design and installation

Q: According to Approved Document M, what is the recommended height range from the finished floor level for switches and socket-outlets in new dwellings?
ANSWER: Between 450mm and 1200mm.
EXPECT: AD P 1.4 New dwellings

Q: What mounting height is suggested for consumer units in new dwellings so they remain out of reach of young children while remaining accessible?
ANSWER: Switches between 1350mm and 1450mm above floor level.
EXPECT: AD P 1.4 New dwellings

Q: When additions or alterations are made to an existing electrical installation, is there a general requirement to upgrade the entire existing installation?
ANSWER: No, only the new work, unless it adversely affects the existing installation or the existing installation can't safely support it.
EXPECT: AD P 1.6 Additions and alterations to existing electrical installations

Q: Under regulation 12(6A), what three specific types of electrical installation work are classified as notifiable?
ANSWER: A new circuit, replacing a consumer unit, and additions or alterations to circuits in a special location.
EXPECT: AD P 2.5 Notifiable work

Q: How is a special location defined horizontally in a room containing a bath tub or shower tray?
ANSWER: 0.6 m from the edge of the bath or shower tray, or 1.2 m from the shower head if there is no tray.
EXPECT: AD P 2.5 Notifiable work

Q: Are domestic outbuildings like conservatories, porches, greenhouses, garages, and sheds exempt from Part P if they share electricity with a dwelling?
ANSWER: No, they must comply with Part P.
EXPECT: AD P 2.3 Scope: where Part P does not apply

Q: What are the three alternative procedures that can be used to certify notifiable electrical installation work?
ANSWER: Registered competent person, registered third-party certifier, or building control body.
EXPECT: AD P 3.1 Certification, inspection and testing: General

Q: Within how many days must an installer or their registration body provide a copy of the Building Regulations compliance certificate to the occupier under the self-certification procedure?
ANSWER: Within 30 days of completion.
EXPECT: AD P 3.3 Self-certification by a registered competent person

Q: How is extra-low voltage defined in Appendix A?
ANSWER: Not exceeding 50V ac or 120V ripple-free dc.
EXPECT: AD P Appendix A: Key terms

Q: Which standard is referenced in Appendix B as the IET Wiring Regulations 17th Edition?
ANSWER: BS 7671:2008 + A1:2011.
EXPECT: AD P Appendix B: Standards referred to

## natural

Q: Hey Dave, if I'm adding a brand-new socket in the middle of my living room wall, do I actually need to notify anyone about it?
ANSWER: No, additions to existing circuits outside special locations are not notifiable.
EXPECT: AD P 2.7 Non-notifiable work

Q: Dave, what happens if I decide to replace my old consumer unit? Is that something I can just do quietly without telling building control or getting certification?
ANSWER: No, replacing a consumer unit is notifiable work.
EXPECT: AD P 2.5 Notifiable work

Q: Quick question Dave, if someone isn't a registered competent person, can they still use a registered third party to certify notifiable work instead of going through building control?
ANSWER: Yes, a registered third-party certifier is an alternative to building control.
EXPECT: AD P Main changes in the 2013 edition
EXPECT: AD P 3.5 Certification by a registered third party

Q: If I'm doing a bit of electrical work in a garden shed, does Part P apply if it gets its electricity from the house?
ANSWER: Yes, outbuildings that share electricity with a dwelling are not exempt.
EXPECT: AD P 2.2 Scope: where Part P applies
EXPECT: AD P 2.3 Scope: where Part P does not apply
